Munnar sits around 1,600 metres up, where three rivers meet and the British planted tea in the 1880s. The estates are still working farms, so the hills look combed: tight green rows broken by silver oak and the odd red-roofed factory.

## What to do

- **Walk the estates early.** Mist usually lifts by nine. Ask your homestay which estate roads are open to walkers.
- **Eravikulam National Park** is home to the Nilgiri tahr, a mountain goat found almost nowhere else. The park closes for a few weeks around February and March while the young are born.
- **A tea factory visit** shows the whole process from leaf to packet in under an hour.

## Good to know

The south-west monsoon (June to August) is beautiful but very wet, and landslides can close roads. The famous neelakurinji flowers turn the hills blue only once every twelve years; the next bloom is expected around 2030.
